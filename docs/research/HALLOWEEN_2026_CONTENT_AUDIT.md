## Current finding

All five live campaign quizzes are logic/rule-application activities. Across 40 tasks, the dominant work is numerical conversion, comparison, threshold classification, ordering or applying a printed fictional rule. Multiple controllers change the appearance more than the mental task. None of the five develops substantive Halloween history, folklore or horror-film knowledge. The ghost/phantom/pumpkin/fog vocabulary largely supplies decoration.

The most serious risk is a promise mismatch: a visitor expecting an alibi mystery, eerie music or a mini-golf experience receives clock calibration, frequency ratios or score arithmetic. Correct scoring and accessible controls cannot by themselves make that content satisfying. These activities can still serve a clearly positioned logic-practice audience; their usefulness in that context does not establish suitability as the Halloween campaign's main attraction.

## Editorial gates for remaining Halloween authoring

1. **Promise and payoff:** the title and first task must deliver the same experience. An alibi quiz needs an actual mystery; a sound quiz needs a meaningful auditory distinction.
2. **Theme test:** explain why the Halloween context changes what the player discovers or decides. Replacing “ghost” with “widget” should not leave the whole experience unchanged.
3. **Distinct mental work:** vary the facts, discoveries, choices and reasoning across eight tasks. Switching from matching to a grid does not count as a new idea if the same supplied rule supplies every answer.
4. **Collection balance:** avoid another calculation-dominated batch. Logical and original fictional experiences remain welcome when they offer a coherent story, meaningful agency, observation or a rewarding deduction. No blanket claim that all math or all game rules are boring.
5. **Earn the effort:** introduce the minimum context needed and provide a worthwhile reveal. Technical disclaimers and long rule cards are not entertainment value.
6. **Research and trust:** source specific factual claims; label fictional scenes and reconstruction accurately. Originality/provenance alone is not factual authority, cultural depth or quality. Separate search demand, seasonal relevance, evergreen appeal and actual engagement.
7. **Human choice and accessibility:** keep descriptive preferences free of diagnostic/moral labels; preserve text alternatives, keyboard access and uncluttered interactions. A meaningful user choice must not be replaced by arbitrary scoring.
8. **Release review:** pass the existing exact-eight-task, section, format, factual/scoring, accessibility, independent-review and deployment gates. Add an explicit audience-fit verdict explaining why a visitor would want to finish and share. Quality takes precedence over filling a daily quota.

Published banks and the five historical calendar rows remain intact. Daily category rotation, five-per-day cap and no-backfill policy remain intact. Remaining brief hooks must be revised under these gates before authoring/release; a KEEP verdict does not waive them.
