# Science & Nature: 25 new quizzes

[Catalog index](README.md) · [Delivery plan](../QUIZ_EXPANSION_250_PLAN_2026-10-18.md) · [Evidence ledger](SOURCES.md)

Proposed concepts, October 18, 2026. All require newly authored question banks and separate slugs. Seeds illustrate tasks; they are not fact-checked answer keys. Wave A = category pilot; B = next four; C = next ten; D = final ten. Dependencies override wave priority. Most target 3–6 minutes; actual durations require playtesting. Research references support rationale, not factual answers.

## SN-01 · Backyard Evidence Lab

**Wave A · Type: knowledge · trend-adjacent · Build: base**

Treat a patch of ordinary ground like a case file and decide which clues actually support which explanation.

**Rounds:** 4 clue sets: choose the best-supported explanation → 4 field notes: order observe, question, test → 3 pairs: match clue to likely cause.

**Example task:** In a labeled fictional observation table, sheltered boxes have more woodlice across repeated counts. Which conclusion is supported, and which would require an experiment?

**Result:** Raw score plus a one-line reason for each miss; no percentile.

**Audience:** Curious teens and adults; North America; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `backyard-evidence-lab`.

**Evidence:** [R07](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Source every answer from museum, university extension or research-institution field guides; review for region-neutral wording, no handling of wildlife, and no unsafe outdoor tasks. Use clearly labeled synthetic observation tables or cited real datasets; distinguish correlation from cause.

## SN-02 · Moon Mission Control

**Wave B · Type: knowledge · trend-adjacent · Build: base**

Make the calls mission planners face, from launch to splashdown, and see why each step comes before the next.

**Rounds:** 3 launch calls: order the steps → 3 transit problems: pick the best response → 3 return puzzles: match constraint to reason.

**Example task:** Why might a mission plan a path that loops the Moon and heads home, rather than orbiting first? Pick the best reason.

**Result:** Mission log score with a short explanation of each decision; flags that this is a simplified model.

**Audience:** Space-curious teens and adults; US/Canada focus; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `moon-mission-control`.

**Evidence:** [R11](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Author only from NASA/CSA public materials, cite each answer internally, avoid claims about real crew events beyond sources; expert review for orbital simplifications.

## SN-03 · Scale Shock

**Wave B · Type: knowledge · evergreen · Build: base**

Put surprising things in size order and find out which of your instincts about big and small hold up.

**Rounds:** 5 sets: order four items smallest to largest → 4 pairs: choose which is larger and by roughly how much.

**Example task:** Order the four labeled specimens by their supplied measured diameters; then compare them with a scale bar.

**Result:** Raw score plus a scale ladder showing every item in its true order.

**Audience:** Anyone who likes surprising comparisons; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `scale-shock`.

**Evidence:** [E00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Verify each size against a museum or research-institution reference with explicit units and ranges; avoid comparisons where definitions vary. Give concrete specimen measurements; broad ranges for hair, sand and cells can overlap.

## SN-04 · Weather Without the Forecast

**Wave B · Type: knowledge · evergreen · Build: base**

Read the sky, wind and air clues yourself and predict what is coming before any app tells you.

**Rounds:** 3 sky clues: choose the likeliest change → 3 chains: order clue, cause, outcome → 3 scenarios: pick what to watch next.

**Example task:** Given the displayed pressure trend, cloud sequence and model assumptions, which of the listed changes best fits the evidence?

**Result:** Raw score plus a clue-to-outcome chain for each answer; not a forecast tool.

**Audience:** Hikers, gardeners, weather fans; North America; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `weather-without-forecast`.

**Evidence:** [E00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Source from NOAA or national weather service education material; regional review (Canada, US, Mexico); never present quiz output as safety guidance. Include uncertainty and all required evidence; no real-weather forecast or safety guidance.

## SN-05 · Mystery Material

**Audience:** Makers, students, tinkerers; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `mystery-material`.

**Evidence:** [E00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Source property statements from engineering or museum materials references; avoid trade-name claims, and use reasoning only, with no experiments requiring heat or chemicals.

## SN-06 · Build a Food Web

**Wave C · Type: logic · evergreen · Build: layers**

Wire up who eats whom in a habitat, then predict how a missing species ripples through it.

**Rounds:** 3 habitat grids: link species to food sources, many-to-one → 3 disruption grids: predict first and second effects.

**Example task:** The supplied food web shows fish eating grazers and birds eating fish. Remove fish: which direct feeding links disappear?

**Result:** Web completed, links correct, plus a ripple diagram of the three effect layers.

**Audience:** Nature fans and students; North America habitats; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `build-a-food-web`.

**Evidence:** [E00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Each habitat needs ecologist review of links and ripple claims; use partial credit; limit to well-documented relationships. Show a simplified explicit web; distinguish direct link loss from uncertain ecosystem-wide population predictions.

## SN-07 · Where Was the Light?

**Wave C · Type: logic · experimental · Build: visual**

Read the direction and length of shadows to work out where the light is and what time it could be.

**Rounds:** 4 controlled shadow diagrams: select the light direction using stated geometry → 2 controlled diagrams: choose the change in light elevation matching the shadow-length change.

**Example task:** Two equal-height posts stand on level ground under one distant light. Use the supplied compass directions to locate the light from their shadows.

**Result:** Scenes cracked with a light-angle explanation; no deterministic ranking.

**Audience:** Visual thinkers, photographers, teens; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `where-was-the-light`.

**Evidence:** [X00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Needs original illustrated or rendered scenes with alt text and a text-only fallback; verify geometry with a science reviewer. No time-of-day inference without date, latitude and orientation; these rounds test explicitly supplied geometry.

## SN-10 · Cause Chain Courtroom

**Wave C · Type: logic · experimental · Build: layers**

Judge a three-step cause chain and find the link where the argument is weakest.

**Rounds:** 4 grids: match each link to its supporting mechanism → 2 grids: find the broken link, many-to-one allowed.

**Example task:** More leaf litter, then more decomposers feeding, then what change in soil nutrients? Which link needs the strongest evidence?

**Result:** Chains judged, with a weakest-link note on each; no fabricated percentiles.

**Audience:** Debaters, students, careful thinkers; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `cause-chain-courtroom`.

**Evidence:** [X00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Use only chains with sourced mechanisms; each link validated independently to avoid compounding errors; reviewer must confirm no step is oversimplified.

## SN-23 · Predict, Reveal, Revise

**Wave D · Type: knowledge · experimental · Build: branch**

Make a provisional scientific prediction, reveal a new observation, and decide whether the evidence changes your explanation.

**Rounds:** 3 experiments: make an unscored initial prediction from limited evidence → 3 reveals: select the conclusion supported by the completed dataset.

**Example task:** Two explanations fit the first observation. A new labeled experimental result rules one out. Which explanation remains supported?

**Result:** Three final evidence decisions and a private before/after prediction timeline; no penalty for reasonable initial uncertainty.

**Audience:** Friends, families, classrooms; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `predict-reveal-revise`.

**Evidence:** [X00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Use clearly labeled synthetic datasets or cited real data. Fixed evidence reveals; score only conclusions supported by the final supplied evidence.
