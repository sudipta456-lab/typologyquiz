# October 18 interactive quiz release

250 new playable quizzes, 25 in each of ten categories, with exactly eight authored tasks each (2,000 tasks). Every quiz has two or three sections and at least two interaction formats. Branching attempts can show fewer than eight tasks. Existing questions, scoring, URLs and progress namespaces are preserved.

Public entry: [Interactive quizzes](https://typologyquiz.com/quizzes/). Canonical banks: `content/interactive/{PC,FS,MT,MU,GT,SN,HC,FE,SG,LW}.json`. Brief identity registry: `content/interactive/briefs.json`. The original research briefs remain historical; the actual banks and generated catalog define the shipped experience.

The player supports choices and dilemmas, weighted ranking, prerequisite ordering, reusable-target matching, many-to-many link maps, grid answers, confirmed sliders, original diagrams with equivalent labeled choices, original browser-generated tone sequences with transcripts, optional reflections and conditional paths. Social rounds can support two to four anonymous players on one device, with a handoff screen and comparison at the end. Controls use native inputs and move buttons; this release does not implement pointer drag-and-drop, video clips, a timed challenge engine or a live multiplayer connection for the new collection.

There are 58 preference, 17 hybrid, 61 logic, 18 cooperative and 96 knowledge quizzes. Knowledge task credit is normalized per task, with partial credit where appropriate; false graph links lose credit and ordering accepts every valid prerequisite-respecting sequence. Preference scales summarize the player's selections without norms, clinical interpretation or assessment-validity claims.

## Research, provenance and review

[Source ledger](SOURCES.md) distinguishes recent North American attention signals from actual quiz engagement. These 250 original quizzes have no measured engagement history yet. Factual reference URLs and original-case provenance are listed in each bank, with question-level source IDs (408 source records across the collection). Fictional maps, scenes, datasets and simplified rules are labeled and supplied in the tasks.

All 2,000 tasks received a separate Claude Code Opus content/logic review after Sonnet authoring. Corrections were adjudicated against the engine and source packs; 163 revised tasks were independently rechecked, followed by the final composition changes. The final read-only code review found no blocking issues and confirmed fixes for partial-answer advancement, player-to-player slider leakage, audio limits, reflection length, branch counts and analytics versions. See [review receipt](CONTENT_REVIEW_2026-10-18.json). These reviews do not establish psychometric validity.

The two AI concepts are private guided reflections: no model is called, no generated interpretation is promised, and reflection text is never persisted. The two survey concepts use explicitly fictional data or the player's own choices; there is no invented respondent sample or fabricated popularity claim. Original tones and schematic diagrams avoid unlicensed recordings or photos. No news edition or prediction event is added.
