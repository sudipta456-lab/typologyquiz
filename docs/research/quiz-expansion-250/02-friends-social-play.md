# Friends & Social Play: 25 new quizzes

[Catalog index](README.md) · [Delivery plan](../QUIZ_EXPANSION_250_PLAN_2026-10-18.md) · [Evidence ledger](SOURCES.md)

Proposed concepts, October 18, 2026. All require newly authored question banks and separate slugs. Seeds illustrate tasks; they are not fact-checked answer keys. Wave A = category pilot; B = next four; C = next ten; D = final ten. Dependencies override wave priority. Most target 3–6 minutes; actual durations require playtesting. Research references support rationale, not factual answers.

## FS-01 · The Group Plan Rescue

**Wave A · Type: cooperative · experimental · Build: preference**

A fictional crew's outing is collapsing under rain, lateness and a tiny budget, and your group has to patch it together.

**Rounds:** 4 plan disasters: choose the fix your group prefers → 4 constraints: order the rescue steps → 3 trade-offs: keep or cut one item.

**Example task:** Rain cancels the park, two people are late and $40 remains. Choose: move indoors, split into two mini-plans, or postpone the main event.

**Result:** Rescue Plan card of your fixes plus a playful pair: Quick Pivot vs Careful Patch. Not a verdict on anyone.

**Audience:** Friend groups, classmates and families planning outings; solo preview works. **Proposed slug:** `group-plan-rescue`.

**Evidence:** [X00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Author all scenarios; no real-person data. Review holiday and food examples for cultural breadth. Pass-one-device flow, no accounts.

## FS-02 · Silent Consensus

**Wave B · Type: cooperative · experimental · Build: social**

Everyone orders the same items in secret, then the reveal shows where your group naturally lines up.

**Rounds:** 4 lists: privately order four items → 4 pairs: pick one without discussing → 2 trios: choose the item most others might pick.

**Example task:** Order these for a lazy Sunday: long walk, board game, nap, group cooking. Then guess which one the others placed first.

**Result:** Agreement map showing aligned and split items with a label like Same Wavelength. No winners, no rankings of people.

**Audience:** Small groups of friends or teammates who opt in together. **Proposed slug:** `silent-consensus`.

**Evidence:** [X00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Needs new cross-player agreement scoring. Keep items neutral and non-sensitive. Pilot by passing one device with a hide-answer screen between turns.

## FS-03 · Pack the Group Picnic

**Wave B · Type: logic · experimental · Build: layers**

Fit snacks, gear and seating to a fictional group's needs when one bag may cover several people.

**Rounds:** 2 grids: match items to people, many-to-one allowed → 2 grids: assign leftover gear to sharing pairs.

**Example task:** One cooler, one blanket, a shade tent and four guests with different needs. Which guests can share the tent without breaking a clue?

**Result:** Score with partial credit per grid plus a short explanation of which clue unlocked each match.

**Audience:** Puzzle fans, planners and friend groups solving together. **Proposed slug:** `pack-group-picnic`.

**Evidence:** [X00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Needs many-to-one matching with partial credit. Author and test each grid for a unique solution. Review dietary examples for inclusivity.

## FS-04 · Rebuild the Weekend Together

**Wave B · Type: cooperative · experimental · Build: sequence**

Put a scrambled fictional weekend back in workable order using time, travel and energy clues.

**Rounds:** 3 timelines: order six activities with constraints → 3 swaps: pick the activity that fixes a conflict.

**Example task:** Brunch is at 11, the hike needs two hours of daylight and the movie starts at 7. Order the day so nobody rushes.

**Result:** Rebuilt-weekend card with raw score and one line explaining each conflict you resolved.

**Audience:** Friend groups and roommates who like planning puzzles. **Proposed slug:** `rebuild-weekend-together`.

**Evidence:** [X00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Uses existing order controls. Author clues so each timeline has one defensible solution. Avoid assuming a weekend schedule common to all cultures.

## FS-05 · Two Truths and a Plot Twist

**Wave B · Type: cooperative · experimental · Build: base**

Spot the invented twist among three claims about a fictional character, then try writing one for your friends.

**Rounds:** 6 character cards: pick the twist among three claims → 2 build rounds: choose a believable twist for a new card.

**Example task:** Mira’s dossier says she repairs watches, keeps no pets and won a pie contest. Which claim contradicts it: clockmaker, three cats, or pie winner?

**Result:** Score with a short reveal of each card's hidden twist and the clue that gave it away.

**Audience:** Party groups and classrooms; works one device or solo. **Proposed slug:** `two-truths-plot-twist`.

**Evidence:** [X00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** All characters and answers are original fiction, written by us. No real facts or people. Test clue fairness so twists are inferable.

## FS-06 · Who Chose That?

**Wave C · Type: preference · experimental · Build: social**

Everyone makes secret picks about a fictional road trip, then the group guesses who picked what.

**Rounds:** 5 pair choices: pick privately → 5 reveal rounds: guess which player chose each option.

**Example task:** Pair: sunrise start or midnight drive. Choose yours, then guess which seat at the table picked the other.

**Result:** Guess tally plus a playful split such as Bold Picks vs Cozy Picks. Not a ranking.

**Audience:** Consenting friend groups of three to six. **Proposed slug:** `who-chose-that`.

**Evidence:** [X00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Needs new multi-player pick-and-guess scoring. Players must opt in; keep prompts light. Do not claim forced choice measures personality scientifically. Requires new preference scoring; never use correct/incorrect credit for taste choices. Requires separate participant state and controlled reveals even when passing one device; add preference aggregation only where explicitly defined.

## FS-07 · Friendship Time Capsule

**Wave C · Type: cooperative · evergreen · Build: social**

Seal group predictions about future plans and open them together on a date you choose.

**Rounds:** 5 predictions: choose likely outcomes for the group → 3 wishes: pick shared goals to seal → 1 reveal: compare when unlocked.

**Example task:** By next spring, will our group trip happen: on schedule, rescheduled twice or turned into a day outing?

**Result:** Sealed capsule card with unlock date; after opening, matches and surprises, never scored as friendship quality.

**Audience:** Friend groups who like keepsakes and slow reveals. **Proposed slug:** `friendship-time-capsule`.

**Evidence:** [E00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Needs asynchronous storage, invites and unlock timing. Requires consent, privacy review and easy deletion. Avoid sensitive prompts about relationships.

## FS-08 · The Everyone-Wins Tournament

**Wave C · Type: cooperative · experimental · Build: social**

In a bracket where every matchup ends with a different shared win, the group builds one celebration.

**Rounds:** 4 matchups: choose a winning category for both sides → 3 semifinals: combine two wins into one plan → 1 final: pick the closing ritual.

**Example task:** Matchup: best snack versus best playlist. Name a category in which both win, such as shared dessert or road-trip pairing.

**Result:** Trophy card listing every win created, with a playful pair like Hosts vs Cheerleaders.

**Audience:** Groups wanting a low-stakes, no-loser game. **Proposed slug:** `everyone-wins-tournament`.

**Evidence:** [X00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** New bracket and scoring flow. Category options authored to avoid ranking people. Review wording so no outcome implies exclusion. Requires separate participant state and controlled reveals even when passing one device; add preference aggregation only where explicitly defined.

## FS-14 · Spot the Unspoken Assumption

**Wave C · Type: cooperative · experimental · Build: base**

Rescue a fictional group conversation by finding the one missing detail that prevents everyone from understanding the same plan.

**Rounds:** 4 fictional exchanges: identify the unstated date, place or responsibility → 4 reply choices: select the message containing every explicitly required detail.

**Example task:** A message says “meet Friday at six,” but the friends are in two time zones. Which missing detail prevents a shared clock time?

**Result:** Eight communication repairs with the missing assumptions revealed; no judgment of real friendships.

**Audience:** Anyone who hosts or attends small gatherings. **Proposed slug:** `spot-unspoken-assumption`.

**Evidence:** [X00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Use closed fictional exchanges and stated communication goals. Accept equivalent complete replies; do not score politeness or personality.

## FS-15 · Pass the Plot Pen

**Wave C · Type: cooperative · experimental · Build: branch**

Each player picks the next story beat for a shared tale, and the ending changes with the group.

**Rounds:** 4 branch points: choose the next story beat → 3 twists: pick how the group reacts → 1 ending: choose the final line.

**Example task:** The map leads to a locked greenhouse. Next beat: climb the roof, find the key under a pot or knock on the door.

**Result:** Story recap of your chosen path with its ending title; a keepsake, not a score.

**Audience:** Game-night groups and classrooms. **Proposed slug:** `pass-the-plot-pen`.

**Evidence:** [X00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Needs branching journey tooling. Author all branches and endings in-house; test every path for tone. No real-person data.

## FS-25 · Everyone Gets Something Swap

**Wave D · Type: logic · experimental · Build: base**

Trade items among fictional friends so each person ends up with what they wanted.

**Rounds:** 3 offer maps: connect each offered item to the person who wants it → 3 exchange checks: select the simultaneous swap satisfying every stated need.

**Example task:** Ana offers a scarf and wants a book; Ben offers a book and wants gloves; Cy offers gloves and wants a scarf. Find the simultaneous exchange.

**Result:** Six completed exchanges with each closed cycle explained.

**Audience:** Logic fans and group-puzzle players. **Proposed slug:** `everyone-gets-something-swap`.

**Evidence:** [X00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Uses one-to-one match and order controls. Author grids with unique solutions and playtest before release. Use simultaneous exchanges, not an impossible pairwise trade order. Every item needs a stated owner and recipient.
