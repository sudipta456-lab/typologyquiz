# History & Culture: 25 new quizzes

[Catalog index](README.md) · [Delivery plan](../QUIZ_EXPANSION_250_PLAN_2026-10-18.md) · [Evidence ledger](SOURCES.md)

Proposed concepts, October 18, 2026. All require newly authored question banks and separate slugs. Seeds illustrate tasks; they are not fact-checked answer keys. Wave A = category pilot; B = next four; C = next ten; D = final ten. Dependencies override wave priority. Most target 3–6 minutes; actual durations require playtesting. Research references support rationale, not factual answers.

## HC-01 · Museum of Everyday Objects

**Wave A · Type: knowledge · evergreen · Build: base**

Read wear, shape and context like a curator to work out what ordinary things were for.

**Rounds:** 4 object cards: choose the most likely use from clues → 4 trios: order objects by when they were commonly used.

**Example task:** A museum record describes a perforated rotating drum that separates flour from coarse bran. Which job fits: sifting, washing, or roasting?

**Result:** Raw score out of 8, with each answer separating what the object shows from what curators infer.

**Audience:** Curious teens and adults; North America; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `museum-of-everyday-objects`.

**Evidence:** [E00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Use objects with documented museum or archival provenance. Source every answer, avoid single-inventor claims, and have a second reviewer check regional variation.

**Example task:** Which already existed before the first iPhone launched in 2007: sending a photo from a camera phone, or this named later app?

**Result:** Score out of 9 plus a timeline strip showing which clue settled each scene.

**Audience:** Teens and adults; North America; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `before-the-first-iphone`.

**Evidence:** [E00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Use the explicit 2007 product milestone, not a vague smartphone era. Source first availability dates and distinguish invention from mainstream adoption.

## HC-04 · Fact, Source Claim, or Interpretation?

**Wave B · Type: knowledge · evergreen · Build: layers**

Sort what a record proves from what someone claims or a historian infers, then fix an overreaching label.

**Rounds:** 3 statements: sort as fact, source claim, or interpretation → 3 sources: choose what each can and cannot prove → 3 labels: pick the wording the evidence supports.

**Example task:** An unsigned diary claims workers leaving caused the mill closure. Does that excerpt verify causation or only document the diarist’s explanation?

**Result:** Score out of 9 with a note explaining each fact, claim, or interpretation call.

**Audience:** Students, teachers and history fans; North America; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `fact-claim-interpretation`.

**Evidence:** [E00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Use invented or well-documented cases. Historian reviews every fact versus interpretation split and shows uncertainty where experts disagree.

## HC-05 · A City in Three Eras

**Wave B · Type: knowledge · evergreen · Build: layers**

Match clues from street life, buildings and trades to the right era of one invented city.

**Rounds:** 4 clues: match transport clues to one of three eras → 4 clues: match buildings and trades to eras → 4 clues: choose what changed between eras and why.

**Example task:** In an invented port city, match 'horse tram', 'grain elevator' and 'ferry dock' to Era 1, 2 or 3; several clues may share one era.

**Result:** Score out of 12 plus a three-era city strip showing your matches and the clue behind each.

**Audience:** City lovers and history fans; North America; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `city-in-three-eras`.

**Evidence:** [E00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Invented composite city inspired by general port-city patterns. Urban historian checks era plausibility; no real neighborhood claims; many-to-one matching needs partial credit.

## HC-08 · Trade Routes Behind a Breakfast

**Wave C · Type: knowledge · evergreen · Build: layers**

Follow the ingredients on one plate back through the trade links that carried them.

**Rounds:** 4 breakfast items: match each to a trade-link story → 4 links: pick why an ingredient became common locally.

**Example task:** Using a cited route map, connect an ingredient’s documented cultivation region, a named trade port and a dated destination.

**Result:** Score out of 8 plus a plate map showing multiple routes behind each item.

**Audience:** Curious adults and students; North America; globally playable. **Proposed slug:** `trade-routes-behind-breakfast`.

**Evidence:** [E00](SOURCES.md).

**Production:** Plant and trade histories have many contributors. Food historian or botanist reviews each answer; avoid single-origin claims; acknowledge labor and exploitation where relevant. Define location and time for every link. Do not sort all ingredients into mutually exclusive native/traded/adopted buckets.
