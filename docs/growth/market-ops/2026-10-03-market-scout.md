# Market-intelligence opportunity cards — 2026-10-03

## 1. October skywatching as a short classroom or library warm-up

- **Audience:** science teachers, school librarians, youth-program coordinators, and local astronomy-club educators planning October activities.
- **Source:** NASA’s [October 2026 Skywatching Tips](https://nightsky.jpl.nasa.gov/planner/) identifies Saturn’s October 4 opposition, the October 21–22 Orionid peak, and the Moon’s October 27–28 pass near the Pleiades. NASA JPL’s [education library](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/edu/) also currently lists K–12 space activities.
- **Why it may matter:** the calendar creates a concrete, time-bounded reason for a five-minute planet-recognition activity before or after a skywatching session.
- **Verified matching route:** [Planets Quiz](https://typologyquiz.com/trivia/planets/) returned HTTP 200 on October 3.
- **Helpful angle:** offer a one-question warm-up prompt that educators can use without linking: “Which planet is at opposition this month?” Then offer the quiz only if the organiser asks for a self-directed follow-up activity.
- **Safe next step:** Publisher relations may identify one local astronomy-club or library programme page with an October skywatching event and its published contact rules. Do not contact NASA or post under its material; do not claim the quiz is a NASA resource or educationally validated.

## 2. Space-lesson companion for educators who want a low-stakes activity

- **Audience:** teachers and informal STEM educators looking for a short transition activity around solar-system sequence, scale, or observation.
- **Source:** NASA’s [Schoolyard Solar System](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/education/schoolyard_ss/) describes a student scale-model activity and identifies planet order, size, distance, and large-number concepts. NASA JPL’s [Find Planets in the Sky](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/edu/resources/project/find-planets-in-the-sky/) is a current student project for grades 2–11.
- **Why it may matter:** these activities include hands-on work; a brief recall quiz can be positioned as an optional opening or closing prompt rather than a replacement for instruction.
- **Verified matching route:** [Planets Quiz](https://typologyquiz.com/trivia/planets/) returned HTTP 200 on October 3.
- **Helpful angle:** state plainly that the quiz is a casual practice activity and invite educators to check every answer against their own lesson materials.
- **Safe next step:** Scout a public educator-resource roundup that explicitly welcomes outside, free supplementary activities. Publisher relations should only approach a named editor or programme organiser after confirming their submission policy and a specific fit.

## Shared lesson for publisher and community roles

The strongest current public hook is October skywatching, but the useful contribution is the prompt or activity design, not a promotional link. Keep any language factual, optional, and free of learning-effect claims. The live physics and alien-interest routes are not suitable substitutes for factual curriculum practice because they are preference quizzes, not knowledge checks.
