### Topic backlog for the weekly picks

The monthly theme determines most of the work. Pick from these four strands as evidence and authoring capacity allow. A strand can provide an entry quiz, a follow-on, and an application without committing to three new pages where existing quizzes already fit.

| Month | Four topic strands |
| --- | --- |
| December | Balanced pushes and steady motion; gear and lever decisions; heat flow in handles and sleeves; switches and broken paths. |
| January | How air takes up space; how mixtures can be separated; where a dissolved solid goes; why bubbles need a second clue. |
| February | Radio reply timing; a planet passing in front of a star; satellite paths; compare a meteor shower with a single streak. |
| March | Beak shape and reach; background and camouflage; movement in plants; compare two habitat counts with the same survey effort. |
| April | Cliff layers and gaps; what earthquake stations measure; fossil preservation; water pathways under a town. |
| May | What a control changes; what a calibration can correct; why averages hide patterns; which next observation would settle a disagreement. |

For longer sessions, combine a 5-minute starter and a new transfer case with a pause to explain the evidence in your own words. For fast sessions, open with one visible puzzle and let players reach a satisfying answer without watching a video or joining a group. Where the catch-up already covers a strand, curate it into the path first and author a new case only for the missing rung.

Check [NASA meteor dates](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/meteors-meteorites/meteor-showers/) and [NASA eclipse visibility](https://science.nasa.gov/eclipses/future-eclipses/) close to publication. Keep the daily target and start by filling two or three slots per week with science inside the existing cadence. Review the mix monthly before allocating more. Reserve difficult material for later steps, with a short worked explanation after a wrong answer. Avoid repetitive tasks that change only the numbers, decorative stories, or a solution disclosed before the player reasons.

## Social play, visual design and release

- Start with labeled text and simple diagrams if a spatial relationship matters. Diagram and text offer identical evidence. Check the smallest supported layouts with keyboard and touch, especially labels, selecting an answer, replay and saved progress.
- Use the existing result panel for spoiler-free canonical links, editable previews and device-generated story or post cards. Scores are optional on existing controls. Keep the share image understandable without player details.
- Use existing pass-and-play mode for cooperative puzzles with separate observer roles and a clear handoff. A family or classroom can also project a solo quiz and discuss the next step. Remote group mode has separate content. The live events hub already offers a Night Sky host guide for an introductory round, and the trivia and movie registries supply the group trivia lobby.
